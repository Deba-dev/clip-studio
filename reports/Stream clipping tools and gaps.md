# Win where Twitch Auto Clips stops

Clip Studio should not try to win on finding moments, because Twitch is giving that away. It should win on what happens around the moment: a local recording the streamer owns, Twitch, YouTube and Kick chat on one timeline, a fast review-and-trim desk, and Hindi/English captions at a flat price in rupees. The category is crowded and cheap to enter. Paid cloud clippers cluster around $15-29 a month, free local capture is everywhere, and Twitch announced free Auto Clips built on chat, voice and on-screen signals ([Twitch](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2026/05/30/everything-we-announced-at-twitchcon-rotterdam-2026/)). Low-star reviews centre on ordinary execution failures, not missing inventions: billing and credits, wrong or dull clips, and slow or failed processing. The problems nobody appears to have tackled, such as music checks, sensitive-information checks, merged multistream chat and arcs across streams, have little or no demonstrated demand, so they should support the product and not define it. A local build is feasible on a streamer's Windows PC if all heavy work waits until after the stream. Local-first is not a business model on its own, though: Powder offered local analysis and still ran out of money. All of this rests on evidence with real holes, chiefly no first-hand Reddit data, no independent clip-quality test, and no confirmation of Auto Clips' current status.

## The evidence shows what surfaced, not how often it happens

Read every ranking below as a signal to test, not a measurement. Reddit, G2, Capterra, Discord and YouTube comments could not be read, so the complaint evidence comes almost entirely from Trustpilot and Product Hunt ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/eklipse.gg?stars=1&stars=2)). Those pages were filtered to low stars, and Trustpilot attracts people chasing refunds, so review counts show what appears on review sites and not true frequency. The review quotes came through a summarising fetch tool and need a spot check before reuse. Eklipse's sites blocked direct fetching, so its prices and features come from search excerpts. TikTok's developer pages, Twitch's help site and the body text of Twitch's legal pages also failed to load. Nearly every "test" of clip quality was published by a competitor, and most time-saving figures come from sellers of automation. "Not found" throughout means one or two searches plus vendor pages. Help documentation for StreamLadder, OpusClip, Vizard and Medal went unread, so a feature could exist there. Revenue, user and word error rate figures are self-reported unless stated otherwise.

## Clipping tools fuse cheap signals, then rank a shortlist

Every tool runs the same pipeline: get footage, score moments, pick candidates, reframe to 9:16, caption, then publish. The research literature supports fusion and ranking over any single detector. On professional League of Legends broadcasts, chat alone scored an F-score of 43.2 against 72.2 for video and 74.7 for both combined ([Fu et al., 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.08559)). A later study found **audio the strongest single signal at 0.366 mean average precision**, fusion at 0.462, and smoothing the score curve at 0.511. In the same study a ranking model reached F1 0.521 against 0.343 for a thresholded classifier ([Ringer et al., 2022](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/186731/1/Highlight_Journal_MLwA_2_.pdf)). Chat still earns its place when there is enough of it. LIGHTOR used three cheap features over a 25-second window (message count, length and similarity), learned a 23-27 second lag between event and reaction, and reached Precision@10 of 0.82 from a single training video ([LIGHTOR, 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.12201)).

None of those numbers transfers cleanly to Clip Studio's users. They come from produced esports broadcasts with casters, or from channels with 800-4,300 chat messages per video. I found no study of low-chat channels, no 2026 measurement of chat delay on any platform, and **no published accuracy figure for any commercial clipper or for LLM ranking of streamer transcripts**. Game-event models are stronger but costly. One cross-game vision model hit 94.3% accuracy on five shooters, fell to 69.0% kill accuracy on an unseen game, and cost about 13 FPS in Fortnite with about 1,500 MB of VRAM ([arXiv 2505.07721](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2505.07721)). For unsupported titles, vendors reportedly fall back to audio intensity plus chat velocity, which misses quiet plays ([AutoClip, secondary](https://autoclip.dev/blog/gaming-clip-tool)). That fallback matches what reviewers complain about.

Footage access is the part most constrained by platforms. I found no official full-VOD download API on any of the three. Twitch's official route covers clips of 5-60 seconds with broadcaster or editor authorisation ([Twitch API](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/api/reference/)). YouTube's API policies bar downloading or storing audiovisual content without approval ([YouTube](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies)). Kick's terms bar scraping and automated copying, and its docs show no clip or VOD endpoints ([Kick Terms](https://kick.com/terms-of-service), [Kick docs](https://docs.kick.com/)). The notes do not establish how cloud clippers fetch VODs, or on what authorisation, so that remains an open question and not a finding. VODs also expire: Kick keeps replays for 7 days on regular channels and 30 on verified ones ([Kick Help](https://help.kick.com/en/articles/7112432-kick-vods-stream-replays)), and Twitch reportedly keeps them for 7-60 days by tier ([StreamVault, third-party](https://streamvault.gg/how-long-twitch-keeps-vods/)). Muted VODs hurt detection too: Eklipse's help page reportedly says muting severely affects AI accuracy ([Eklipse, search excerpt](https://eklipse.gg/help/does-a-muted-vod-prevent-ai-highlights/)). A local recording sidesteps downloading, expiry and post-hoc muting at once. Kick makes live ingest close to mandatory as well, because one developer and one open-source project say its past chat is unavailable, though Streams Charts claims otherwise ([DEV](https://dev.to/devil_scrapes/kick-chat-scraper-archive-live-chat-before-it-disappears-forever-32b3), [Streams Charts](https://streamscharts.com/tools/chat-logs-downloader)).

The back half of the pipeline is simpler than vendors suggest. Gaming reframing is mostly a layout problem: find the fixed webcam rectangle once, then stack it above gameplay, as open-source clipsmith does ([clipsmith](https://github.com/ricardogr07/clipsmith)). Speaker tracking matters mainly for IRL footage. Captions typically use Whisper-family speech recognition with voice-activity gating and word timestamps ([faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)). Gating matters on gaming footage because non-speech audio makes Whisper hallucinate words ([Calm-Whisper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12969)). No word error rate benchmark on gaming speech was found.

A glossary source says its clip suggestions are English-only with 16:9 output ([Creator Essentials](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/auto-generated-clips/)).

Two events define the business reality. **Powder closed**: powder.gg now redirects to a notice saying the team "can no longer sustain the app" after stretching its finances ([Powder notice](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1p8TjN7bCRdOnhrD5DjG02pmoGi4ylsGKmdCS-YV4jrg/edit?tab=t.0)). The notes disagree here. One treats the closure as unconfirmed, because Steam still lists the app ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1794970)), but the notice itself is direct evidence and outweighs a stale listing. Only the date stays unresolved. Powder is also the closest prior art for Clip Studio's core idea. It reportedly offered unlimited free local analysis ([Toolmage](https://www.toolmage.com/en/tool/powder-1/)), with chat spikes among its signals ([TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/20/powder-an-ai-clipping-tool-for-gaming-can-detect-when-a-creator-yells-during-a-stream)). The second event is **Eklipse dropping its free AI tier** in June 2026, which suggests that cloud AI minutes are hard to give away ([Eklipse, search excerpt](https://eklipse.gg/help/is-eklipse-free)). Read together, the two events show cloud clippers squeezed by compute cost and at least one local tool squeezed by monetisation. One closure is a caution, not a rule, but "no per-minute compute cost" does not by itself produce revenue.

The claim that local processing plus chat is unoccupied needs narrowing. I found no live commercial product doing both, since local tools rely on hotkeys or game events and chat-aware tools appear to be hosted. Two open-source projects sit close, however. clipsmith ranks by chat density and `!clip` signals before an LLM pass (MIT, 1 star) ([clipsmith](https://github.com/ricardogr07/clipsmith)). ClipsKitty combines audio heuristics, a transcript pass and an LLM rerank (AGPL-3.0, 213 stars) ([clips-studio](https://github.com/ColinGPT9/clips-studio)). So the honest claim is "no polished, supported product found", not "nobody has done this". ClipsKitty's repository is named `clips-studio`, which is a naming collision, and its AGPL licence means its code cannot be borrowed for a closed app.

Money in the category flows mostly elsewhere. OpusClip reportedly made $20M in 2025 ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/opusclip/)), though trackers range from about $10M to an unsourced $30M+. Medal's clips now feed General Intuition, reported at a $6.2bn valuation, with an opt-out for players ([MobileGamer](https://mobilegamer.biz/general-intuition-raises-220m-at-6-2bn-valuation-trains-ai-on-gameplay-clips/)). No credible market size exists for stream clipping specifically, and I found no revenue data for Eklipse, StreamLadder or Vizard.

## Twitch Auto Clips is the central risk, and its status is unknown

Twitch announced in May 2026 that Auto Clips would generate captioned clips from chat activity, vocal inflection and on-screen events, moving to beta "in the coming months". It said only 50% of streamers have a clip after a stream, rising to up to 85% with Auto Clips ([Twitch](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2026/05/30/everything-we-announced-at-twitchcon-rotterdam-2026/)). That is the same signal stack as paid tools, free, with first-party access to video, chat and viewer behaviour. Twitch also plans to use viewer reactions on clips as "strong signals", which no third party can match. The 85% is Twitch's own figure and measures having a clip, not having a good one, but having any clip is the exact job a small streamer would otherwise pay for.

**Current status is unconfirmed.** Twitch's help article would not render. A July 2026 third-party post says the voice command starts in English for Partners and Affiliates ([BadgeBase](https://badgebase.de/blog/twitch-auto-clips/)), and another third party claims Auto Clips is live ([StreamChat AI](https://streamchatai.com/blog/twitchs-new-ai-stream-summaries-and-auto-clips-what-they-actually-mean-for-your-channel)). The voice command and Auto Clips are separate features, so English-first evidence for one does not settle the other. A search summary reports an English-only alpha ([streamer.guide](https://streamer.guide/blog/twitchcon-rotterdam-2026-new-features-1440p-clips-guilds)), while the official post gives no language detail. Plan on the assumption that the English limit is temporary.

The table is my judgement of what Auto Clips erodes and what survives, assuming it reaches most Twitch streamers.

| Capability | What the evidence says | Durability for Clip Studio |
|---|---|---|
| Finding moments on Twitch | Announced with the same signal families | None: treat as a commodity |
| Captions on Twitch | Announced | None for English; language quality is the only edge |
| Having any clip on a small channel | The stated goal of the feature | None |
| Vertical reframing | Auto-crop "being explored"; [vertical clip editor reported](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/twitch-rolls-out-new-clip-editor-to-convert-live-streams-into-vertical-videos); Dual Format in beta | Short-lived |
| Hindi and other non-English | Weakly sourced English-first rollout | Temporary; depends on model quality, not access |
| Kick and YouTube streams, multistream chat | Twitch-only by nature | Durable against Twitch, exposed to YouTube's own tools |
| Full-quality local file, long cuts, any lead-in length | Twitch's API clips run 5-60 s; Auto Clips' own length limits are unknown | Durable |
| Work that survives without any platform or subscription | Not a platform incentive | Durable |

Three decisions follow. First, **no Clip Studio pitch should rest on "finds your best moments on Twitch"**. English Twitch-only streamers are welcome users but the worst primary target. Second, the wedge audience is PC streamers on Kick and YouTube, those multistreaming, and non-English creators. Twitch cannot serve the first two groups at all, and may lag for the third. Third, treat Auto Clips as a possible input. Twitch's clip APIs allow editors to list and download clips ([Twitch API](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/api/reference/)), but whether Auto Clips output appears there is unknown.

The steelman against building is that Twitch's move signals the whole layer going native, with YouTube following. That risk is real, and it lands on the India thesis in particular. If Indian streamers skew towards YouTube, which the notes do not establish, a Hindi-capable YouTube highlights feature would hit harder than anything Twitch ships. The counter is that native tools optimise for clips on their own platform. No platform has an incentive to deliver a cross-platform file workflow that sends the streamer's best moments to rivals.

OpusClip and Vizard are reported to charge by source minute ([CreateSocial](https://createsocial.ai/blog/opus-clip-pricing)), credits are consumed on retries, and work vanishes when plans lapse ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/opus.pro?stars=1&stars=2)). Small creators report free tiers that show clips they cannot export.

**The Medal rows are the warning for local-first.** They are the only local-app complaints in the sample: startup lag and frame hitches, 659 GB of unwanted recordings, corrupted app state, and "malware-like" behaviour ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/medal.tv?stars=1&stars=2)). Eight reviewers is a small number, but running on the streamer's PC means inheriting exactly these failure modes. A local app that hurts a stream or fills a disk loses more trust than a slow website does.

Why streamers give up remains unanswered. Vendor estimates of manual work range from 5-15 minutes per clip ([Snowball](https://snowball-for-streamers.com/en/blog/best-twitch-clip-software)) to 1-2 hours of editing per hour streamed ([FrostyTools](https://frostytools.com/blog/how-to-turn-twitch-vods-into-clips-without-editing/)). All are sellers' figures, and they measure different things. No first-hand account of quitting short-form was found. Likewise, no direct user evidence shows low chat breaking detection. Major tools use audio and game signals too, so low chat removes one input and does not disable the tool. The defensible version is narrower: a quiet, low-chat, non-shooter stream loses most signals at once.

Some open gaps are open for good reason. Music checking is technically hard: Chromaprint is tuned for clean music, AcoustID's free service is non-commercial, and background-music matching shows low recall across tested systems ([AcoustID](https://acoustid.org/webservice), [ISMIR 2022](https://ismir2022program.ismir.net/static/posters/228.pdf)). Even Mubert calls its result "a risk estimate", so a clean result must never read as "safe". The sensitive-information evidence has a logic gap as well. The cited incidents, such as the Kai Cenat and IShowSpeed address leak, happened live ([Dexerto](https://jamtest.dexerto.com/entertainment/fans-swarm-kai-cenat-ishowspeed-after-address-leaks-on-stream-2194832)), so a clip scanner would not have prevented them and could only limit further spread. Multistream merging has no demand evidence at all. It costs little only because Clip Studio must ingest live chat from each platform anyway. Whether anyone would pay for a safety step is unknown.

One platform rule does justify a low-cost music feature right away. YouTube blocks any Short over one minute with an active copyright claim globally, while Shorts under a minute escape that rule ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877)). A default of under 60 seconds when music is present is a design rule, not a detection problem.

## A post-stream local pipeline fits on ordinary hardware

**Capture.** The obs-websocket v5 protocol covers replay buffer control, record events, file splitting and chapters ([protocol](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/obsproject/obs-websocket/master/docs/generated/protocol.md)). The safest design rotates recordings with `SplitRecordFile`, listens for `RecordFileChanged`, and reads only closed segments, because I found no statement that a file can be read while OBS writes it. Require OBS 32.0 or later, which made Hybrid MP4 the default for new profiles and fixed splitting bugs ([OBS 32.0](https://obsproject.com/blog/obs-studio-32-0-release-notes)). Keep markers in the app's own database, because Hybrid MP4 chapters do not survive a crash ([OBS KB](https://obsproject.com/kb/hybrid-mp4)). Three things remain untested: whether a split drops frames or audio, how audio tracks map in Hybrid MP4, and what a full local recording adds to encoder load during a stream. Streamlabs Desktop and other broadcast apps were not researched.

**During the stream, stay on the CPU.** NVIDIA now lists 12 concurrent encode sessions on consumer cards, though older drivers may enforce less ([NVIDIA](https://docs.nvidia.com/video-technologies/video-codec-sdk/13.1/nvenc-application-note/index.html)), so session count is not the constraint. Contention for the GPU is. I found no measurement of Whisper running beside a game plus NVENC. The nearest data point is the vision model above, which cost about 13 FPS. Live work should be limited to logging chat, loudness and hotkey markers, plus optionally YAMNet audio tagging (521 classes, about 3.7M weights) ([YAMNet](https://github.com/tensorflow/models/blob/master/research/audioset/yamnet/README.md)), at below-normal priority with zero VRAM. A governor should read OBS stats and pause analysis if frames skip. Queued segments mean a pause loses nothing.

**After the stream, processing takes minutes.** faster-whisper on an RTX 3070 Ti transcribed 13 minutes of audio in 17 seconds batched (large-v2 fp16, 6,090 MB VRAM) and 16 seconds at int8 (4,500 MB). The small model on an i7-12700K took 51 seconds batched ([faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)). The table derives 4-hour estimates, excluding overheads such as VAD and decoding.

| Hardware | Setup | 4-hour stream, derived | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPU only, 12700K class | small int8, 1.5-3.6 GB RAM | About 16-32 min | Measured on a 13-min clip |
| RTX 3070 Ti 8 GB | large-v2 class, batched or sequential | About 5-20 min | Measured on a 13-min clip |
| RTX 3060 or 4070 12 GB | large-class int8 or fp16 | Unmeasured; [one secondary source](https://www.promptquorum.com/de/power-local-llm/local-whisper-stt-comparison-2026) gives about 12x on a 4070 | Weak |
| GTX 1660 6 GB | turbo or fine-tune at int8 | Unmeasured; VRAM fits on paper | Weakest |

Even the CPU row beats the multi-hour and multi-day cloud waits reviewers describe, if it holds up on real streams. One open question is that the README does not say whether batched mode supports word timestamps, which captions need, so the fast figures may not apply to the caption pass.

**Hinglish is the model decision that matters most.** Stock Whisper large-v3 scored 29.74 word error rate on a conversational Hinglish set, against 13.67 for a maintainer's preview fine-tune ([Trelis preview](https://huggingface.co/Trelis/whisper-hinglish-preview)) and 14.41 for Tara ([Tara](https://huggingface.co/Trelis/tara)). All of this is self-reported on clips of 30 seconds or less, and the same maintainer's two pages disagree on commercial comparators (Scribe-v2 on HiACC appears as 16.98 and as 12.87), so the ranking is not settled. The preview's weights "may change". Tara's page lists Apache-2.0, but the other note did not verify that, so confirm it. A product risk sits underneath these numbers. The preview outputs each language in its native script, and Tara's output script is unconfirmed, while Hinglish captions are usually read in Roman script, which is the researchers' judgement and not a sourced finding. Parakeet and Canary lack Hindi, and Qwen3-ASR's aligner does not cover it ([Qwen3-ASR](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B)). Real streams with game audio will score worse, so a clean mic track is worth asking for.

**Ranking should work with no LLM.** Bin signals at one second, compare each against a rolling channel baseline, shift chat back by a per-channel lag, smooth, then take top peaks. An optional text-only cloud pass on about 20 candidates can add reranking and titles. Sending a whole 4-hour transcript of about 70,000 tokens costs roughly 2-12 US cents on [Gemini's listed prices](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing), by my estimate, and candidate windows cost far less. Keep it opt-in and use a paid tier, since Google's free tier may use content to improve its products. Local 8B models need 6-9 GB of VRAM after the stream, speed reports conflict from 18 to 47 tokens per second ([SpecPicks](https://specpicks.com/reviews/ollama-vs-llama-cpp-vs-vllm-rtx-3060-12gb-2026)), and nothing shows they rank Hinglish moments well.

**Packaging and licences.** The research recommends staying on Python, pywebview, PyInstaller and Inno Setup, with the worker in a separate process so native crashes spare the UI. The biggest size decision is avoiding PyTorch at runtime, which adds 2-5 GB ([PyTorch forum](https://discuss.pytorch.org/t/pytorch-install-is-that-large-5-gbs/213629)). CTranslate2, ONNX Runtime and first-run model packs in `%LOCALAPPDATA%` replace it. Ship an LGPL FFmpeg as a separate process with no x264 or x265, and encode with NVENC, AMF or QSV ([FFmpeg licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/master/LICENSE.md), [BtbN](https://github.com/BtbN/FFmpeg-Builds)). Whether a given LGPL build includes libass and hardware encoders must be confirmed by inspecting it. Three legal items are unresolved and need review: H.264/HEVC/AAC patents in a paid app, FFmpeg's official compliance checklist (the page was unreachable), and NVIDIA's redistribution terms for CUDA libraries.

**Three platform frictions sit behind a local-first label.** First, the notes assume Kick chat arrives by webhook, and a desktop app has no public address, so this probably needs a small relay. Kick's terms make unofficial connections risky ([Kick Terms](https://kick.com/terms-of-service)). This is my inference and needs an early spike. Second, YouTube caps most stored API data at 30 days ([YouTube policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies)), and a Twitch agreement clause quoted in a 2025 forum thread limits caching ([Twitch forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.com/t/storage-of-data-from-twitch-api/63884)), so long-lived raw chat logs should be reviewed. Keeping derived signals and deleting raw messages is the conservative option. Third, publishing is uneven:

| Platform | Key limit | Fit for a local app |
|---|---|---|
| [YouTube Shorts](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost) | 100 `videos.insert` calls a day per project across all users; [unverified projects no longer forced private since 2026-11-02](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/revision_history) | Good at launch; needs an audit at scale |
| [TikTok](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/content-sharing-guidelines) | `SELF_ONLY` until audited; bars app watermarks; rejects utility uploaders. Official pages unreadable, so this comes from excerpts | Plan upload-to-inbox after audit |
| [Instagram Reels](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/) | Professional accounts; media generally fetched from a public URL; permission review | Poor: likely needs hosting |

TikTok's watermark rule has a commercial consequence: a "made with" watermark cannot be the free-tier lever for API-posted content. YouTube's reused-content policy can strip monetisation from a whole channel and applies even with permission ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)), so an auto-post-everything design is a liability. Per-clip human review is both the safe design and the product.

## Conclusion: sell the review desk and ownership, not the detector

Detection is becoming a free commodity, and it is also the part least proven by any evidence here. What the sources do show is a set of trust failures: charges for bad output, waits with no answer, work deleted on lapse, and apps that hurt the PC. A solo developer cannot out-detect Twitch, but can beat a cloud queue on turnaround and beat a platform on portability. That shifts the design centre from the AI to the minutes after a stream ends, when a streamer scans a shortlist, fixes a trim and exports. Powder's closure supplies the matching commercial lesson: the free tier must leave a real reason to pay, so charge for ongoing value and do not rely on local compute savings to carry the business.

India is the sharpest wedge and the least evidenced. I found no stream-clipping tool with confirmed INR and UPI pricing, and Cut.Pro is running the same regional playbook in Brazil, though its results are unknown ([Cut.Pro](https://cut.pro/en/blog/best-clipping-tool-twitch-kick-2026)). A vendor guide says most foreign merchants cannot accept UPI QR or collect requests ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/best-ai-video-generator-india-2026)), which would favour an India-based seller. But the notes hold no count of Indian streamers using these tools, no willingness-to-pay data, and nothing on how many stream from a PC. BGMI support is unconfirmed anywhere, and phone-only or console-direct streamers cannot use an OBS-based app at all. Test that before treating the wedge as a market.

### Product differentiators

Ranked by how well the evidence supports them. The first four are the pitch, and the last three are supporting proof.

| # | Differentiator | Concrete form | Evidence for demand | Why a rival struggles to copy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Results minutes after the stream, no queue | Whole-stream analysis finishes on the user's PC, with a visible progress estimate | Demonstrated (20 reviewers), Trustpilot only | Cloud tools pay per minute and queue under load |
| 2 | Flat price, no credits, nothing expires | No per-minute billing; projects and files stay usable after a plan lapses | Demonstrated (28 and 5) | Credit models follow cloud cost structures |
| 3 | A review desk built for fixing | Ranked shortlist with stated reasons, drag trims with undo, transcript-based lead-in, any length from the local file | Demonstrated (7, plus setup complaints) | Platform clips are short and tied to their platform |
| 4 | Hindi and Hinglish first, in rupees | Fine-tuned local speech model, Roman-script captions, UPI checkout | Moderate for non-English; none measured for India | Needs local language care and an Indian billing setup |
| 5 | Works for quiet streams | Mic-track, laughter and transcript signals outrank chat, with per-channel baselines | Demonstrated complaint; the solution is unproven | Twitch uses voice too, so this edge must be shown in tests |
| 6 | One timeline across Twitch, YouTube and Kick | All live chat merged against one local recording | None found; a side effect of required ingest | Twitch cannot do this; hosted tools could add it |
| 7 | Respectful local app and pre-post flags | No footage leaves the PC by default, no footage used for training, disk budget, clean uninstall; music flag steers Shorts under 60 s | Thin (Medal complaints); music demand weak | Medal's data value runs the other way |

Do not claim better AI, "the only local clipper", or "copyright safe". The evidence supports none of these.

### Recommended v1 scope

The v1 target is a PC streamer using OBS on Kick, YouTube or several platforms at once, with Hindi/Hinglish speech as a first-class case. V1 does one job: **stream normally, then get a ranked shortlist minutes later, fix it, and export 9:16 clips with captions**.

| Area | In v1 | Deliberately out of v1 |
|---|---|---|
| Capture | OBS 32+ connection, replay-buffer hotkey, full recording with rotation, own marker log, disk budget | Own capture engine, other broadcast apps, VOD downloading of any kind |
| Live work | CPU-only logging of chat, loudness and hotkeys; governor that pauses on skipped frames | GPU analysis during streams, live auto-clipping, live exports |
| Chat | Twitch EventSub and YouTube `streamList`; Kick once the delivery spike clears | Chat replay imports, third-party clipping of others' streams |
| Detection | Post-stream audio, YAMNet events, transcript and chat, top-K ranking with per-channel baseline | Per-game models, facecam emotion, taste learning |
| LLM | Works without one; opt-in text-only cloud rerank and titles | Bundled local LLM by default |
| Captions | Stock turbo model plus one Hinglish fine-tune, VAD on, editable transcript, bundled Devanagari-capable font | More Indian languages, translation |
| Editing | Trim, undo, lead-in extension, facecam rectangle set by drag or from OBS scene data | Full timeline editor, active-speaker tracking for IRL |
| Export | FFmpeg LGPL with hardware encoders, one master render, save to folder | Software x264 path |
| Publishing | YouTube Shorts upload with per-clip review | TikTok and Reels APIs, scheduling, auto-posting |
| Safety | Music-presence flag from YAMNet, under-60 s default | Fingerprinting, on-screen text scan (first post-v1 candidate) |
| Hardware | NVIDIA path first, CPU fallback | AMD and Intel GPU acceleration until NVIDIA is tested (Vulkan is the later route) |
| Business | Free tier with real, unwatermarked exports capped by volume; flat Pro plan in INR via UPI and USD by card | Credits, lifetime deals, unlimited free analysis with no cap |

Pricing has no willingness-to-pay data behind it, so treat this as a starting hypothesis. Test about ₹399-499 a month in India, anchored on ChatGPT Go at ₹399 in a 2025 report ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/chatgpt-rolls-out-india-pricing-upi-payments-now-accepted)) and below FluxNote's ₹999 ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/for-india)). Elsewhere, test $7-9 a month, between Cross Clip's $4.99 and the $15 entry tier of OpusClip. Pro should earn its price through unlimited exports, Hinglish captions, Shorts upload and an included rerank allowance. Powder's reported $9.99 Pro ([Toolmage](https://www.toolmage.com/en/tool/powder-1/)) is a reminder that a low price alone guarantees nothing.

Six checks should come before committing to this scope, in this order, because each can change it. First, look at Auto Clips directly on a real channel and in Twitch's help pages: who has it, which languages, what clip length, and whether its clips reach the API. Second, read the named subreddits by hand and interview about ten PC streamers on Kick and YouTube, including Hindi speakers, to replace review-site inference with real demand and to learn whether they already record locally. Third, build a labelled set from real streams, including quiet and low-chat ones, and measure whether the ranker beats simple loudness. If it cannot, differentiator 5 goes. Fourth, run the performance matrix on real hardware: game capped and uncapped, OBS with NVENC, analysis off and on, logging OBS skipped frames and 1% lows, plus split-boundary gaps and reads of growing files. Fifth, test Hinglish models on stream audio after conversion, covering Roman-script output, word timestamps and the licence. Sixth, clear the legal and naming items: FFmpeg compliance, codec patents, CUDA redistribution, Kick chat delivery, and the Clip Studio name. Beyond the `clips-studio` repository, from general knowledge outside the notes, Clip Studio Paint is an established product, so a trademark check belongs here.
